About the lab

Optimizing recovery after a brain lesion

Octopus Lab studies how the brain recovers and adapts after a stroke. Our main focus is post-stroke aphasia, a language disorder that limits how people communicate with family, work and take part in social life. Speech-language therapy helps, but it rarely leads to full recovery.

We test whether pairing therapy with non-invasive brain stimulation (rTMS and tDCS) can strengthen recovery, and we use EEG and fMRI to see what the stimulation changes in the brain. Our goal is to make brain stimulation a practical, personalized part of rehabilitation.

The questions we ask

  • Which brain region should we stimulate for each person?
  • What changes in the brain after brain stimulation in post-stroke aphasia?
  • Why do some people respond to stimulation better than others?

Why an octopus

The octopus

It is one of the most intelligent animals in the ocean. It observes, explores, solves problems and remembers. Its intelligence is not only in its head: about two-thirds of its neurons are in its tentacles.

Yet nothing is passed on. A young octopus does not receive teaching nor shared knowledge. Each generation receives a remarkable brain, but no instructions. So every octopus starts over, and learns what others already knew.

Aphasia

After a stroke or a brain injury, aphasia can feel the same. A familiar word no longer comes. A sentence breaks before it ends. People have to find new paths and rebuild links.

It can feel like starting over. But if the words are missing, the thoughts are still there, the emotions, the intelligence and the relationships.

Our mission

We support this new start. We study how the brain reorganizes, and help preserved networks work around broken pathways, building new possibilities for language and connection, one connection at a time.

Like the octopus, the brain explores, compensates and invents. It does not always return to the old path. Sometimes it creates a new one.

Starting over does not mean failing. It means learning differently, building on what remains, and repairing.

Even when language breaks, the brain keeps its ability to search, to learn and to reinvent itself.

Community

Bringing people with brain injury together

Recovery does not happen only in the clinic. Octopus Lab is building a community that brings together people living with an acquired brain injury, including stroke and traumatic brain injury (TBI), along with their families and caregivers.
